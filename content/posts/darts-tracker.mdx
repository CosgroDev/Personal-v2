I've been playing darts for years. A couple of seasons ago I became captain of my team, which put me in charge of a decision I'd always found frustrating when someone else made it: picking the lineup.

The old way was gut feel. Who played well last week? Who's been on form? The problem with gut feel is that it tends to weight the most recent thing you can remember, not the full picture across a season.

I do data analysis for a living. I spend my days in food safety and quality, turning factory performance numbers into stories that people can understand and act on. Applying that same thinking to a Tuesday night pub league felt like an obvious thing to do.

So I built an app.

## What it does

Anyone in the team can log a game after they've played — opponent, score, result. Over time it builds up a form picture for everyone across the season. When I'm picking the team for the next match, I'm looking at actual performance data, not whatever happened to stick in my head.

The scorer is interactive, so you can track a leg as you're playing it rather than just recording the final result. It means the data is granular enough to actually be useful — not just wins and losses, but how people are performing under pressure, how consistent they are, where they're dropping shots.

## Why it works

It's not complicated. But having numbers behind a selection decision changes the conversation entirely. Nobody argues with the data the same way they'd argue with a captain's hunch.

More than that, it's made me a better captain. I'm not just going on who I think is playing well — I'm looking at who *is* playing well, week in, week out, across a full season.

The same thing that makes me useful at work — finding the signal in the noise and turning it into a decision — turns out to work just as well in a pub league. I don't think that's a coincidence.

Once you start thinking in systems, you see them everywhere.
