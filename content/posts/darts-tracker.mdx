I've been playing darts for years and took over as captain earlier this year. One of the first things I noticed was that we were relying on other people's tools to track results. There are scorers out there, but they're not ours, and the data doesn't stay anywhere useful.

So towards the back end of the season I built something for the team.

The old way was gut feel. Who played well last week? Who's been on form? The problem with gut feel is that it weights the most recent thing you can remember, not the full picture across a season.

I do data analysis for a living. I spend my days in food safety and quality, turning factory performance numbers into stories that people can understand and act on. Applying that same thinking to a Monday night pub league felt like an obvious thing to do.

## What it does

Anyone in the team can log a game after they've played: opponent, score, result. That last part matters. I'm not always there, and if the only person who can log a game is the captain, half the data never gets captured. Anyone can do it, so it gets done.

Over time it builds up a form picture for everyone across the season. When I'm picking the team for the next match, I'm looking at actual performance data, not whatever happened to stick in my head.

The scorer is interactive, so you can track a leg as you're playing it rather than just recording the final result. It means the data is granular enough to be useful. Not just wins and losses, but how people are performing under pressure, how consistent they are, where they're dropping shots.

## Why it works

It's not complicated. But having numbers behind a selection decision changes the conversation entirely. Nobody argues with the data the same way they'd argue with a captain's hunch.

More than that, it's made me a better captain. I'm not just going on who I think is playing well. I'm looking at who *is* playing well, week in, week out, across a full season.

What makes me useful at work, finding the signal in the noise and turning it into a decision, works just as well in a pub league. I don't think that's a coincidence.

Once you start thinking in systems, you see them everywhere.
